What is an ADU?

An Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) is a self-contained residential unit located on the same property as a single-family home. ADUs can be detached, attached, or conversions of existing spaces such as garages or basements. They offer an excellent way to add value to your property, generate rental income, or provide additional living space for family members. With the increasing need for affordable and flexible housing options, ADUs have become a popular solution for homeowners in San Diego looking to maximize their property's potential.

In San Diego, ADUs are more than just a trend; they're part of the city's long-term plan to expand housing options without altering neighborhood character. Recent changes in local and state regulations have made it easier than ever to build these units legally and efficiently. Whether you're thinking about a rental suite, a private space for aging parents, or a guest retreat, ADUs offer flexibility that grows with your family and lifestyle. Plus, with the right design, even a compact structure can feel spacious, private, and fully livable.

Types of ADUs

There's no one-size-fits-all approach when it comes to building an ADU. Depending on your goals, budget, and available space, here are the most common types of ADUs homeowners consider:

Detached ADU

Attached ADU

This type is physically connected to the main house but has a separate entrance, allowing for privacy without a full separation. It's a great option for properties with limited yard space. Attached ADUs are often used as in-law suites, home offices, or transitional housing for adult children.

Transforming an existing garage into a fully functional living space is one of the most cost-effective ways to build an ADU. Garage conversions typically include a kitchenette, bathroom, and open-concept living area. Since the foundation and structure already exist, the project timeline can be shorter.

Basement or Attic Conversion

Repurposing underused interior space is a smart way to create an ADU without expanding your home's footprint. These conversions are ideal for homes in hilly areas like parts of La Mesa or El Cajon, where basements are more common. With proper waterproofing and insulation, these areas can become cozy, rentable studios or private family units.

Junior ADU (JADU)

A JADU is a compact living unit, typically under 500 square feet, carved out of the existing home. It includes a separate entrance, a small kitchenette, and may share a bathroom with the main house. JADUs are a popular choice for multigenerational living or as short-term rental spaces, and they often qualify for simplified permitting in San Diego.

Why Build an ADU?

Whether you're looking to create more space or make the most of your property investment, building an ADU comes with several practical benefits for homeowners in San Diego and surrounding areas:

Increase Property Value

An ADU can significantly boost your home's worth by adding livable square footage and long-term rental potential. In a competitive real estate market like San Diego, this extra unit can make your property more appealing to future buyers or investors.

Generate Rental Income

Many homeowners use their ADUs to bring in passive income through long-term leases or short-term rentals. With high demand for affordable housing in areas like Chula Vista, El Cajon, and Carlsbad, even compact units can rent for competitive rates.

Multi-Generational Living

ADUs provide a smart solution for families who want to keep loved ones close while maintaining privacy. Whether it's aging parents, college-aged kids, or extended family, an ADU offers the right balance of independence and connection.

Flexible Use

Your ADU can adapt as your life changes. What starts as a guest suite can later become a remote office, a creative studio, or a home gym. Some clients even convert theirs into dedicated homeschooling areas or wellness spaces.

Sustainable Housing Solution

With the right design, ADUs can be built using energy-efficient appliances, solar panels, and sustainable materials. These small-footprint dwellings naturally consume less energy and often align with San Diego's green building incentives.

Affordability

Building an ADU is a cost-effective way to meet housing needs without moving, compared to purchasing a new home or upsizing. Many families see it as a strategic long-term investment that pays for itself over time.

Work-From-Home Space

With more professionals working remotely, a detached or private ADU makes for an ideal home office or client-facing workspace. It's a quiet, dedicated zone that separates work from the main living space, boosting focus, productivity, and balance.

What Affects the Cost?

Several factors can impact your final ADU price:

  • Site Conditions : Sloped lots, limited access, or soil issues may increase foundation or grading costs.
  • Size & Layout : A larger footprint or multi-bedroom layout will naturally cost more.
  • Finish Level : Luxury countertops, tilework, cabinetry, and smart tech integration all affect pricing.
  • Permits & Code Requirements : Local regulations vary slightly between cities (e.g., Coronado vs. Del Mar).
  • Utility Connections : Tying into existing water, sewer, and electrical lines can vary depending on layout.

ADU Construction FAQs

How long does it take to build an ADU in San Diego?

Timelines vary based on design complexity and permitting, but most ADUs take 4 to 6 months from start to finish. Our team handles everything, including permits, so there are no delays from miscommunication or outsourcing.

How much does an ADU cost to build?

Costs depend on the type, size, and finish level of your ADU. A garage conversion or JADU can be more budget-friendly, while a detached ADU with luxury finishes will cost more. We'll provide a detailed estimate during your consultation so there are no surprises.

Can I rent out my ADU for additional income?

Absolutely. Many of our clients use ADUs as long-term rentals, guest suites, or short-term stays. We'll help you design a unit that's both functional and attractive to renters.

What's the difference between a JADU and a full ADU?

A Junior ADU (JADU) is a smaller unit, usually under 500 square feet, built within the footprint of the main home. It must have its own entrance and a kitchenette, but shares utilities. A full ADU can be detached, larger, and fully self-contained, offering more flexibility.
